My Beanie

A personal health-record app: lab values, blood pressure, weight and meals logged one line a day, then organized on one page before the visit. The mascot, Beanie, is a mint-green bean character that sets the tone of the app's guidance.

Why Kidney

Five scenes the product started from

Situations that recurred in patient and caregiver interviews. Each one led to a feature.

Scene01Day Zero
"The checkup report said eGFR 54. Nothing hurt."

The kidneys show few clear symptoms until late stage 3. There is a number but no language to explain it, so most people walk past it.

→ This scene became The Self-Explanation Index
Scene02The Three Minutes
"A three-minute visit. I didn't get to ask a single thing."

Three months of life have to fit into a few minutes. The patient, unable to recall them, says "I've been fine", and the clinician sees only the numbers. The two sets of information never meet in the exam room.

→ This scene became The One-Pager
Scene03The Search
"Thirty minutes of searching. All that was left was fear."

Generic health information ends at lists of allowed and forbidden foods. Little of it starts from an individual's numbers and table.

→ This scene became Reverse Content
Scene04The Family
"Ask, and it's nagging. Don't ask, and it's worry."

The person cooking and the person logging are different people. When they cannot see the same information, care turns into surveillance.

→ This scene became Mirrored Care
Scene05Why This Disease
SLOW · SILENT · EXPENSIVE: a condition where all three overlap

It progresses slowly, shows no symptoms, and is costly when caught late. A condition with all three needs a tool that records daily, organizes before the visit, and can be seen together with family.

→ That tool is called My Beanie
Five Choices

Core features

The base screens are the same for every user; the dialysis log is an add-on switched on in Settings.

The One-Pager · MVP

Pre-visit report

Ninety days of labs, blood pressure, weight, body signals and questions on one page. Print it or save as PDF and bring it to the visit. The app never sends the report to clinicians; sharing is the patient's decision.

Time Travel · MVP

Then vs. now

The last labs beside this one, the last 30 days beside these 30. In a chronic condition, no change is also a result worth confirming.

The Quiet Nudge · MVP

One line, never a lecture

A single line at a time. No notification on skipped days; one line only three days before a visit, in a high-blood-pressure week, or when swelling persists.

Mirrored Care · MVP

Caregiver mirroring, the mirror card

The patient picks which items to share (eGFR, blood pressure, weight, body signals, meal patterns, questions) and sends one link. Unselected items are not in the link. Family opens it read-only in the same app. Live sync and the family subscription follow once a server is introduced. Supporter plans →

Meals · MVP

Meals without counting

Breakfast, lunch, dinner and snack, each with a one-line note, a photo, pattern chips, and potassium / phosphorus / sodium caution tags. The patient sets the daily count goal; the app only counts. Reverse Content, swap suggestions that start from what you ate, is planned for v2.

Dialysis Log · Add-on

Dialysis log, switched on in Settings

For users on dialysis only. Pre/post weight per session and the gain between sessions, dry weight, daily fluid intake, symptoms and access-site signals, medication check. When off, it appears nowhere in the app. Read more →

Why these four

Why the MVP took these cells

Two cells come from system 02 because the pre-visit report is the most defensible feature. Comparable U.S. services already present a PDF for the doctor as their core feature, so the quality of this area determines the product's competitiveness.

Autonomy first

Three MVP cells in the Autonomy column

Most early churn comes from the impression that the app is issuing instructions. Competence takes time to build; autonomy is decided on the first screen.

The Self-Explanation Index

Self-explanation: the one value meaningful to patients, clinicians and payers alike

Time-in-app and log counts prove the app is well made — not that the patient is better off.

LV0 · UNAWARE

No words

"I've been fine."

No language for one's own state.

LV1 · AWARE

Records

"The swelling was bad last month."

Facts recovered. Phase 1 goal for most users.

LV2 · COMPARE

Reads

"My creatinine went up."

Language acquired. Phase 2 goal. Pass/fail line for each R&D cycle.

LV3 · CONNECT

Explains

"The week I ate salty food, the numbers moved."

Life linked to numbers. Phase 3 goal. This is where the visit changes.

LV4 · LEAD

Decides

"I'll eat out less this month."

Few reach it, but those few pull the next patient up.

The Regulatory Line

We start as a personal wellness product, not a medical device

Korea's MFDS judges by intended use and risk, and the deciding factor is the wording. The same feature is wellness if it "helps you record" and a medical device if it "slows progression." In the U.S., the FDA explicitly lists tools that let patients record and organize health information as not medical devices.

What we don't doWhat we do instead
Stage the disease from eGFR and raise risk alarmsLet the patient enter, store, and chart the numbers the hospital gave them. eGFR is shown as a reference value only
Tell an individual "cut protein to X grams"General dietary information from authoritative bodies, plus reminders. Individual guidance only within a clinician's prescription or referral
Transmit or analyze patient data for cliniciansThe patient prints their own report and carries it in
Use "diagnose · treat · slow · improve" in marketingConsistent health-management language: record, organize, look together
Rule: no claim reaches the product screen without an Evidence Note. Health data is treated as sensitive: separate consent, end-to-end encryption, and in-region storage are the default design. The current MVP sends nothing off the device.
