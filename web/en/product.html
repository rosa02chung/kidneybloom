Why Kidney

Five scenes — where this product was born

Moments that repeat in patients' lives. Each one became a feature.

Scene01Day Zero
"The checkup report said eGFR 54. Nothing hurt."

The kidneys stay quiet until late stage 3. There is a number but no language for it, so most people simply walk past it.

→ This scene became The Self-Explanation Index
Scene02The Three Minutes
"A three-minute visit. I didn't get to ask a single thing."

Three months of life have to fit into a few minutes. The patient, unable to recall them, says "I've been fine." The doctor sees numbers; the patient lived a life; the two never meet.

→ This scene became The One-Pager
Scene03The Search
"Thirty minutes of searching. All that was left was fear."

Generic information ends at "eat this, don't eat that." Nothing starts from my numbers and my table.

→ This scene became Reverse Content
Scene04The Family
"Ask, and it's nagging. Don't ask, and it's worry."

The person cooking and the person logging are different people, looking at different information — so care turns into surveillance.

→ This scene became Mirrored Care
Scene05Why This Disease
SLOW · SILENT · EXPENSIVE — where all three overlap

It progresses slowly, shows no symptoms, and costs dearly when caught late. A disease with all three needs a tool that records daily, organizes before the visit, and is seen together.

→ That tool is called My Beanie
Five Choices

Five choices

Three are in the app today; two are planned for v2.

The One-Pager · MVP

Pre-visit report

Ninety days of labs, blood pressure, weight, body signals, and questions on one page. Print it or save as PDF and bring it along. The patient holds the report — the app never sends it to clinicians.

Time Travel · MVP

Then vs. now

Last labs beside this one, last 30 days beside these 30. In a chronic condition, holding steady must be shown as an achievement.

The Quiet Nudge · MVP

One line, never a lecture

A single line at a time. No nagging on skipped days. It speaks only before a visit, in a high-blood-pressure week, or when swelling persists.

Mirrored Care · MVP

Caregiver mirroring — the mirror card

The patient picks which items to show (eGFR, blood pressure, weight, body signals, meal patterns, questions) and sends one link. Unchecked items are not in the link at all. Family opens it read-only in the same app. Live sync comes once a server exists.

Reverse Content · v2

Start from what you ate

Not "eat this" — start from what you actually ate and suggest one adjustable thing for today. A swap list, not a ban list.

Why these four

Why the MVP took these cells

Two cells come from system 02 because the pre-visit report is the most defensible feature. CKD Tracker and KidneyPal already advertise "a PDF for your doctor"; lose here and everything is lost.

Autonomy first

Three MVP cells in the Autonomy column

Most early churn comes from the feeling that "this app bosses me around." Competence takes time to build; autonomy is decided on the first screen.

The Self-Explanation Index

The only value that matters to patients, clinicians, and payers at once

Time-in-app and log counts prove the app is well made — not that the patient is better off.

LV0 · UNAWARE

No words

"I've been fine."

No language for one's own state.

LV1 · AWARE

Records

"The swelling was bad last month."

Facts recovered. Phase 1 goal for most users.

LV2 · COMPARE

Reads

"My creatinine went up."

Language acquired. Phase 2 goal. Pass/fail line for each R&D cycle.

LV3 · CONNECT

Explains

"The week I ate salty food, the numbers moved."

Life linked to numbers. Phase 3 goal. This is where the visit changes.

LV4 · LEAD

Decides

"I'll eat out less this month."

Few reach it, but those few pull the next patient up.

The Regulatory Line

We start as a personal wellness product, not a medical device

Korea's MFDS judges by intended use and risk, and the deciding factor is the wording. The same feature is wellness if it "helps you record" and a medical device if it "slows progression." In the U.S., the FDA explicitly lists tools that let patients record and organize health information as not medical devices.

What we don't doWhat we do instead
Stage the disease from eGFR and raise risk alarmsLet the patient enter, store, and chart the numbers the hospital gave them. eGFR is shown as a reference value only
Tell an individual "cut protein to X grams"General dietary information from authoritative bodies, plus reminders. Individual guidance only within a clinician's prescription or referral
Transmit or analyze patient data for cliniciansThe patient prints their own report and carries it in
Use "diagnose · treat · slow · improve" in marketingConsistent health-management language: record, organize, look together
Rule: no claim reaches the product screen without an Evidence Note. Health data is treated as sensitive: separate consent, end-to-end encryption, and in-region storage are the default design. The current MVP sends nothing off the device.
